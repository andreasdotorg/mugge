## F-295: Audible clicks during Mixxx DJ playback on NixOS

**Severity:** High
**Status:** RESOLVED (session 16)
**Found in:** USB isochronous transfer jitter on VL805 xHCI controller
**Affects:** DJ mode audio quality — all DJ playback
**Found by:** owner (session 15, during DJ load test after F-291 RT fix)

### Description

Audible clicks during normal Mixxx DJ playback on the NixOS deployment. The
clicks are continuous during playback — not related to PipeWire restart, mode
switching, or startup transients. This is a distinct issue from F-187 (noise
on channels after multiple PW restarts) and F-016 (glitches after PW restart
with capture adapter).

### Evidence

- 43-minute DJ load test (session 15, post F-291 RT fix)
- pw-top shows +107 Mixxx errors accumulated over the 43-minute session
- PipeWire convolver pipeline itself is clean (~0.07 xruns/min, likely
  startup transients only)
- The errors are concentrated in the Mixxx JACK client, not in the
  PipeWire filter-chain or other audio services

### Root Cause (confirmed)

**USB isochronous transfer jitter on the VL805 xHCI controller** with a
~1ms bimodal WAIT pattern (2.5-2.8ms vs 3.5-3.8ms between transfers). The
ALSA ring buffer at `period-num=4` was only 4096 samples with 448 samples
(9.3ms) of available margin (`avail_max` 3648/4096). This was too tight to
absorb USB timing variation from the USBStreamer's isochronous transfers.
When USB transfer timing skewed beyond the buffer margin, ALSA reported
xruns that PipeWire propagated as ERR counts to downstream clients (Mixxx
JACK client, metering nodes).

**CPU was NOT the bottleneck.** Deep diagnostics showed:
- pw-top -b capture: 29 snapshots over 30s, CPU 16-26% across all cores
- All PipeWire node B/Q ratios < 12% (well within cycle budget)
- cyclictest: max latency 158us (well within quantum deadline)
- The bottleneck was ALSA buffer depth, not processing time

**Evidence — quantum scaling confirms buffer margin pressure:**
- q512: 4.6 ERR/min (tightest budget, most errors)
- q1024: 2.7 ERR/min baseline (confirmed by worker-2 final monitoring; earlier
  0.4/min reading was statistical anomaly)
- q2048: 0.8 ERR/min (most headroom, fewest errors)

**Precursor fix (pcm-bridge node.passive, commit `0e8a960`):** Before the
primary fix, the pcm-bridge capture node was missing `node.passive = true`,
causing it to self-promote to graph driver. This accounted for ~45% of ERR
count. Fixing `node.passive` reduced ERR from ~25/min to ~8.4/min,
revealing the USB isochronous jitter as the remaining root cause.

**Disproven hypothesis:** Initial investigation suspected xHCI USB IRQ cache
pollution on CPU 0 competing with audio threads. While the IRQ load is real
(100M+ interrupts pinned to CPU 0), IRQ affinity pinning did not resolve
the issue. The actual bottleneck was ALSA buffer depth, not CPU contention.

### Resolution

**Fix: `period-num` 4 -> 5** in `configs/pipewire/21-usbstreamer-playback.conf`
(commit `b8584f8`).

Increases ALSA ring buffer from 4096 to 5120 samples. Zero latency impact
(PipeWire quantum unchanged), zero CPU impact. ALSA `avail_max` moved from
3648/4096 (89% utilization, 448 margin) to 4672/5120 (91% utilization, 448+
margin with larger buffer absorbing jitter).

**Result: 94% ERR reduction** (16/min -> ~1/min at q1024 during active Mixxx
playback). Core graph measurement with metering nodes not deployed at time
of test; expected production rate 1-2/min. The residual ERR rate is within
acceptable limits for DJ/PA operation (no audible clicks).

**Fix timeline:**
1. pcm-bridge `node.passive` fix (commit `0e8a960`): 25/min -> 8.4/min ERR (45% reduction)
2. `period-num` 4->5 fix (commit `b8584f8`): 16/min -> ~1/min ERR (94% reduction from pre-fix baseline)
3. Combined effect: ~25/min -> ~1/min

### Key Observations

- F-291 RT fix (SCHED_FIFO/88 for PipeWire) resolved convolver pipeline
  xruns but did NOT resolve the Mixxx clicks — different root cause
- Error rate scales inversely with quantum size, confirming buffer margin
  pressure rather than a software bug
- xHCI IRQ count (100M+) indicates sustained high-rate USB traffic from
  the USBStreamer's 8-in/8-out ADAT stream
- CPU utilization was never the bottleneck — the VL805 USB controller's
  isochronous transfer timing is the fundamental limitation

### Distinction from Other Defects

- **F-187** (Critical): Noise on ALL 4 channels + broken spectrum after
  multiple PW restarts. Different symptom — F-187 is catastrophic noise,
  F-295 is intermittent clicks during normal operation.
- **F-016** (Medium): Glitches specifically after PW restart with capture
  adapter. F-295 occurs during continuous playback, not post-restart.
- **F-291** (RESOLVED): PW at SCHED_OTHER. Fixed — PW now at FIFO/88.
  F-295 persists after F-291 fix, confirming a different root cause.
